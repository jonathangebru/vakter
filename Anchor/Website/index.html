§ what it does

Six things, all the moment
someone threatens it.

§ 03 / 09

Photo burst

The instant the alarm fires, the front-facing camera starts. Three to thirteen frames depending on mode. JPEG to local disk. Pointed at the person holding your Mac.

i

10 s ambient audio

AAC at 22 kHz mono. Voices. Names. The room they walked into. The car they got in. Photos catch a face if the thief is in front of the camera — audio catches everything else.

ii

iMessage + Apple Maps

Your phone gets a message within seconds — the photos and a Maps pin at the last known location. From your own Apple ID via Messages.app. No Vakter server in between.

iii

100 dB siren, 10 voices

Sample-quality sirens, not synthetic beeps. The Mac says "this MacBook is being tracked" in English, Dutch, Norwegian, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Japanese, Korean, or Mandarin — at max volume, with mute overridden.

iv

Police-ready PDF

Single-page report. Device serial. Event timeline. Photo list. Audio paths. Cryptographic chain-of-custody verification. Hand it to a cop. Hand it to an insurance adjuster. They'll read it.

v

Optional cloud bucket

Backblaze B2 or any pre-signed URL. Your bucket. Your keys in your Keychain. The evidence is in the cloud before the thief gets past authentication — so it survives a wipe.

Each mode adjusts grace window, audibility, and what counts as a trigger. The Mac you carry to a coffee shop has different expectations than the Mac you check at the hotel desk.

The privileged daemon has exactly one job: toggle pmset disablesleep while you're armed, so the lid-close doesn't put your Mac to sleep before the alarm fires. Roughly 150 lines of Swift, signed by Apple's Developer ID, audited by macOS at every launch. Verify the entitlements yourself with codesign --display --entitlements -.

§ what we don't do

Three negatives
we never break.

§ 06 / 09
nocloud

Your disk, your evidence.

Photos, audio, event log — all written locally. Vakter does not operate a server. The only thing that goes off-device is the iMessage you configured to send to your own phone.

notelemetry

Zero outbound calls.

No analytics. No "help us improve" beacons. No crash reporters that ship a stack trace home. Activity Monitor and Little Snitch will both show you the same thing: silence.

noaccounts

Nothing to sign up for.

No email. No password. No login. Download the DMG, run it, forget it exists until the day you need it. The Apple ID on the Mac is the only identity Vakter ever sees.

✓ Notarised by Apple · Hardened Runtime · Developer ID Application (9TA5GB5UJH). Audit the binary yourself with codesign --display --entitlements -.

§ FAQ

Reasonable questions.

§ 08 / 09

Why not just use Find My?

+

Find My is excellent for recovery — once the Mac is gone and the thief has powered it back on or connected to Wi-Fi, you can track it. Vakter operates earlier. The instant the lid closes or the cable is pulled, Vakter is already screaming, photographing, and texting your phone.

They're complementary. We recommend running both, and the architecture page is explicit that nothing Vakter does overlaps with Find My's job.

Why is Vakter closed-source?

+

We're a single-developer indie product. Closed-source lets us iterate faster, keep architectural freedom, and avoid the operational tax of running a public repo while hunting for product-market fit.

We compensate with notarisation, Hardened Runtime, a tiny privileged surface (~150 lines), and a public commitment to fund a third-party security audit at $50k MRR. Little Snitch has been closed-source for twenty years and is the gold standard. If you'd prefer fully open-source, LuLu (Objective-See) and Pareto Security are excellent alternatives — they solve adjacent problems.

Does it work if my Mac is wiped?

+

The local evidence — photos, audio, event log — survives on disk through authentication. If the thief can't unlock the Mac, the evidence is there when you recover it. If they do get past authentication, the on-device evidence may be at risk.

Cloud-bucket upload streams evidence off-device during the alarm — to your own Backblaze B2 bucket or any pre-signed URL — so it survives a wipe. Configure it once in Settings → Auto-arm & Cloud.

What if the thief disables Wi-Fi?

+

The local alarm fires regardless. Siren, photos, audio — all written to disk before the network leaves. If Wi-Fi comes back later (and it usually does), the iMessage retries. The local evidence is there for the moment you recover the Mac.

Can it fire accidentally at a coffee shop?

+

That's exactly what Café mode is for. 12-second grace, 30-second alarm cap. The Bluetooth trust radius is configurable — your phone in your pocket, your AirPods in your ears, your watch on your wrist all count as you're still nearby. If a trusted device is in range, lid-close alone doesn't trigger.

What runs as root?

+

One process. VakterPrivilegedDaemon. Its only job is to call pmset disablesleep so the Mac doesn't fall asleep before the alarm fires when the lid closes. Roughly 150 lines of Swift, signed by Apple's Developer ID. Read its Info.plist with codesign --display --entitlements - whenever you want.

Everything else — the state machine, the camera, the microphone, the XPC server — runs as your user. Same privilege level as your browser.

How do I disarm?

+

Unlock your Mac normally. macOS has already authenticated you — Vakter trusts that. There's no second password to remember. Disarming is just opening your Mac like you always do.

You can also disarm from the menu bar or the Settings panel. The iOS and Apple Watch companions are source-complete; provisioning is in progress. Once live they will let you see alarm state and event history from your phone.

macOS and hardware requirements?

+

macOS Sonoma (14.0) or later. Apple Silicon (M1 or later) only. Internal camera, microphone, and speakers. Bluetooth optional (trusted-device awareness). Notarised DMG, direct download — no Mac App Store dance.

Vakter is anti-theft — what does "digital threats" mean?

+

The physical threat is someone walking off with your Mac. The digital threat is someone tricking you into doing their work for them — a web page that silently writes a malicious shell command to your clipboard, then walks you through opening Terminal and pasting it. In 2025, this technique (known as ClickFix) was the second-largest malware loader vector on macOS. Apple added a binary paste warning in macOS 26.1; it blocks without explaining. Vakter v1.6 will intercept clipboard writes from browsers and, before you paste into Terminal, tell you in one sentence what the command actually does.

The watch is the same. The scope expanded.
